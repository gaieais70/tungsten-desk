How we price an unpriced market

Tungsten has no exchange, no central clearing, and since 4 February 2025 — when China placed tungsten under its dual-use export-control regime — the two benchmark assessments (APT 88.5% WO₃ CIF Rotterdam and the Chinese domestic market) no longer form one market. Fastmarkets calls it "fragmenting". That fragmentation is our opportunity: instead of one "true" price, we publish five independent estimates, each with explicit assumptions, and let their divergence itself carry information.

Limitations — read before trading anything

  • The "reported" daily path between verified anchors is an analyst reconstruction, not a published assessment. Only the two ISBP/Dornhofer anchors ($330 open-2025, >$3,000 on 17-Jul-2026), the IMARC carbide figure, and the export-control date are independently verified. The intermediate monthly shape is documented in M0 and is the largest source of model uncertainty.
  • Equity-implied prices are not prices. Tungsten equities carry mine, finance, jurisdiction and dilution risk. The elasticity (β) is calibrated on a single 18-month window and would drift in a new regime.
  • Factor-model R² is low (~0.14). Tungsten is driven by China policy, not copper. The factor path is a sanity band, not a forecast — that is itself a finding.
  • The lead-lag test found a negative 21-day lead correlation between equity excess returns and APT moves — the naive "stocks lead spot" alpha hypothesis is rejected by this dataset. Equities may still add information in a Kalman fusion, but as a contemporaneous noisy signal, not a leading one.
